## Observed techniques

### Named mechanism without a complete explanation

The fragment names a four-part stock-picking filter called CODE and promises a report explaining it. The acronym's components are not visible. A skill using this source can study the naming and packaging, but must not invent what C, O, D, or E stand for.

### Proof near offer objections

Selected stock returns appear before the problem discussion; testimonials and later results reappear before the price. This shows that proof is revisited as the reader encounters a new doubt. The file does not provide a complete portfolio history, methodology, or independent verification.

### Problem → opportunity → report

The inflation discussion creates interest in hard assets. The gold/silver section leads to a buying guide. The farmland section develops a supply/demand argument and leads to another report. Each report is presented as a way to act on a specific idea rather than an arbitrary extra.

### Benefits and usability in the bundle

The offer includes four reports, a monthly issue, member updates, a password-protected site, and subscriber support. The service components answer practical concerns about keeping up with recommendations and getting assistance.

### Refund terms and purchase-flow reassurance

The copy allows cancellation during the subscription and says the buyer can retain received materials. The final CTA explains that the subscription contents can be reviewed before the order is placed. These address different concerns: suitability after purchase and uncertainty before purchase.

## Internal inconsistencies to preserve as review examples

1. The copy calls the twelve-month experience free and later asks for a $59 trial payment. The refund may explain the intended framing, but "free" and an upfront refundable charge are different terms. A new offer should state the charge and refund directly.
2. A late paragraph promises five special reports, while the visible inventory and closing recap list four. Do not silently manufacture a fifth item.
3. Eight "gifts" combine reports with the paid service and its support components. Clearly distinguish the core subscription from bonuses when adapting the bundle.
4. Assertions such as no losing year, cumulative gains, and selected testimonials do not establish a representative customer outcome.
5. A subscription refund does not remove the risk of following investment ideas.

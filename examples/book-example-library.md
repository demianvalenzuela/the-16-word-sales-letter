## Worked promotional passages

### Weekly Paycheck Indicator: a connected argument

Locators: pp. 32-33, 37, 50-53, 59; relevant authority passage pp. 72-73. See the lecture for the underlying options-activity explanation.

- **Opening:** brief speaker identification, a physical patent application, a before-news distinction.
- **Benefit:** a specific weekly-income promise.
- **Obstacle:** entering after news or price changes means arriving late.
- **Shared opposition:** ordinary investors have historically been outside privileged information flows.
- **Mechanism bridge:** a proposed way to get positioned earlier.

Use for consistency between Q1 and Q4. Do not infer an issued patent, proven predictive accuracy, or representative income from the source's claims.

### Congressional Checks: proof with tension

Locator: pp. 44-46. Quotes from media and a tax official are combined with financial-disclosure references and a contrast between lawmakers and ordinary taxpayers.

Use to study evidence arranged around a conflict. The invented promotional label is not necessarily an official program. Quoted authority on tax treatment does not automatically support every payout implication.

### Republican Checks: audience-specific opposition

Locator: pp. 59-60. A tax-law opportunity is framed through the political beliefs Evaldo attributes to his audience, followed by an individual's income and retirement outcome.

Use to understand the connection between existing beliefs and Q5. Do not transfer the political profile to another audience or reuse the historical amount as a verified case.

### Crypto event: urgency built on a forecast

Locator: pp. 63-65. A possible Amazon/Bitcoin announcement on October 26 is connected to a predicted third-stage boom, using a shopping-rush analogy.

Use for diagnosing the distinction between a scheduled event and an uncertain event outcome. The full PDF and breakdown use different dates. See [the full case](eccentric-millionaire.md).

### Penny-stock speaker: I've been in your shoes

Locator: pp. 69-70. Frustration with slow stocks and a modest background are followed by a changed approach and a student's result.

Use to connect shared difficulty to a credible route. Keep the speaker's story, the student's experience, and the reader's possible outcome distinct.

### Wall Street insider: Robin Hood

Locator: pp. 70-72. An affluent derivatives-trading career gives way to dissatisfaction and a decision to share an income secret.

Use for access → motive → sharing. Do not fabricate access, secrets, or moral conflict for a client.

### Chicago trader: expert storyline

Locator: pp. 72-73. The speaker's progression through trading-floor roles and media appearances establishes relevant experience.

Use when expertise is better supported than a transformation story. Connect credentials to the current claim.

### Cash for Patriots: mechanism explanation

Locator: pp. 76-78. Overseas corporate cash, policy changes, and political/media statements are arranged into a repatriation narrative.

Use to study familiar premise → explanation → evidence. Do not treat the promotional label as a government benefit or skip the connection from policy to the reader's actual opportunity.

### Weekend-trading close: three alternatives

Locator: pp. 86-90. The reader can remain where they are, analyze the market independently, or receive filtered research and alerts.

Use for comparing the work and assistance involved. Historical profits, capacity of 250 people, and guarantee claims require separate support in a new offer.

## Worked reconstruction: concrete stake

The narrator emphasizes the repeated physical idea of a single $100 bill. His interpretation is that a familiar object makes the entry amount easier to imagine than an abstract financial commitment.

To transfer the reasoning:

1. Identify the quantity the reader already understands.
2. Explain the proposed change using that quantity.
3. Provide the evidence and conditions for the relationship.
4. Keep a hypothetical outcome labeled as hypothetical.

The provided PDF uses softened "tiny investment" language in several places where his version uses $100. Do not quote one version while citing the other.

## Worked reconstruction: open loop

The letter introduces a three-step script, names a future event, and promises to explain both later. The repeated diagram keeps the question visible until the stages are defined.

The useful principle is continuity of a meaningful question. An open loop fails when the promised reveal never arrives, when the answer is trivial, or when repeated deferral feels like withholding. Essential price or renewal information should not be hidden behind curiosity devices.

## Worked reconstruction: preserving the speaker's voice

The narrator says he listens to Altucher's podcast and recognizes the candid discussion of failures and personal history. The actual PDF includes technical background, public disagreement, financial loss, and recovery.

A new application should separate voice cues from biographical facts. A candid tone can be adapted; another person's life story cannot. Gather the client's real expressions, degree of formality, typical explanations, and approved experiences.

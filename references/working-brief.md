# Working brief and review

This is an operational adaptation of the book and supplied examples. It is not an additional framework claimed to be Evaldo's own.

## Build the short argument

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing details that materially affect the deliverable. Do not require a questionnaire when the brief already answers it.

| Brief field | What is needed |
|---|---|
| Audience and context | Who is deciding, in what situation, with what familiarity |
| Desired change | Outcome and personal significance |
| Prior attempts | Alternatives used, results, and known limitations |
| New opportunity | Meaningful difference in the route to the outcome |
| Mechanism | Actual process and evidence for its important steps |
| Speaker | Relevant history, competence, motive, and voice |
| Proof | Claims, sources, conditions, and limitations |
| Offer | Product/service, delivery, price, bonuses, payment and cancellation terms |
| Timing | Real deadline, event, or consequence of delay |
| Action | What the reader should do next and what happens afterward |
| Language and format | Audience language, channel, length, and requested stage of sale |

Write One Belief. For a full sales letter or VSL, give each of the ten questions a short answer. For an email, ad, landing page, product page, or other format, select the questions relevant to the piece's intended action and the reader's existing context. Beside each consequential answer, record either the supporting evidence or the gap. A draft can retain a clearly marked gap; it should not fill it with an invented testimonial, number, mechanism, or deadline.

## Evidence record

| Claim | Evidence/source | What it supports | What it does not establish | Draft decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A proposed claim | Relevant supplied evidence | Narrow supported conclusion | Missing scope, causality, typicality, or conditions | Use, qualify, replace, or leave a gap |

Use this only at a detail level proportionate to the task. A full financial promotion needs more scrutiny than a short positioning exercise.

## Retrieve examples by function

Select one or two relevant examples from the master index. Read the actual case file before relying on it. Extract:

- The reader concern it addresses.
- The copy move used.
- The evidence that move requires.
- What must change for the current audience.

Preserve the client's product, audience, and requested deliverable. Do not turn every task into a crypto-style newsletter, a long VSL, or a new offer redesign.

## Draft and revise

Use the book's sequence as the planning default for a full sales letter or VSL. For other formats, organize the relevant functions around the intended next action. Let finished sections overlap where the argument needs it. Keep the language natural for the audience. For Spain, use Spanish from Spain when that is the intended market, without mechanically importing American financial-promotion rhetoric.

When improving existing copy, read it in full and preserve the requested format, length, voice, and supported facts. Diagnose the most consequential weaknesses, explain the main changes, and deliver a revised version when improvement or rewriting is requested. A review alone can provide findings and targeted repairs. Do not treat an absent price, guarantee, or full mechanism explanation as a defect when the piece's purpose is to earn a click or reply and those details belong later.

For reviews, report the most consequential failures first:

1. Broken or unsupported One Belief.
2. Missing difference, benefit, or initial proof.
3. Problem/solution mismatch or unsupported blame.
4. Unclear mechanism, trust gap, or speculative urgency.
5. Inconsistent offer, price, guarantee, or CTA.
6. Redundancy, voice, and sentence-level friction.

For each finding, cite the passage, name the question/function, state why it matters, and give a concrete revision. Do not assign invented conversion scores or predict sales uplift without data.

## Final decision checks

Apply the checks relevant to the piece's purpose, format, and stage of the sale. A short ad or sales email does not need to carry the full argument.

- The opening establishes both difference and reader benefit.
- Each central claim has the appropriate support or qualification.
- Q4's obstacle is the one the mechanism addresses.
- Biographical details and testimonials belong to the actual speaker/customer.
- The urgency distinguishes confirmed facts from forecasts.
- The offer's item count, totals, price equivalents, timing, and refund scope agree.
- The next action matches the real user flow.
- Example-derived decisions are labeled as observations or adaptations when attribution matters.

Deliver the requested copy or review. Add only the unresolved assumptions that affect use. Do not attach the full internal worksheet unless it is useful or requested.
